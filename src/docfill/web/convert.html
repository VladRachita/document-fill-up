1. The document to convert

A Word document (.docx, .doc) becomes a PDF; a PDF becomes a Word document (.docx). The result is scored against the original: how close to 1:1 it is, and where it differs.

Engine

.pdf

2. The real document (optional)

The real document

What the converted document should look like: the PDF Word saves, or the Word document a PDF was made from.

The converted document

Made by any program (Word, Adobe, an online converter, docfill…). Word documents are laid out by LibreOffice to compare their pages.

How the scores work

Each check gives 0–100; the score is their weighted mean, over the checks that could be made. 97 and more: 1:1, practically identical; 90: very close; 75: close; under 75: different.

Layout, page by page · 50Both documents are drawn page by page; the share of the ink (text, lines, pictures) found at the same place in both, within 0.7 mm. A missing or extra page counts 0. A Word document is laid out by LibreOffice to be compared.
Text · 30Every word, in its order. From Word to PDF: the words of the document found in the PDF (page headers and numbers printed on every page are not held against it).
Pages · 8The number of pages: of the original PDF, of the real document, or the number Word saved with the document when it last saved it (used when it is up to date).
Fonts · 8The fonts of the text: the same font (100), a font of the same widths such as Liberation Serif for Times New Roman (90: the lines break at the same place), or another font (0).
Pictures · 4The number of pictures kept.
Against the real documentWithout it, a Word document converted to PDF is not compared page by page (LibreOffice would be compared with itself): add the PDF Word saves for the true 1:1 score.
